USDC redemption pressure: the public layer

Thales Barbosa · onchain data engineering · data through 13 Oct 2026

The FSB's October 2026 note on global stablecoins asks authorities to monitor secondary-market liquidity and redemptions. Both are already public on Ethereum. This page reads them for the weekend Silicon Valley Bank failed, then for the last 90 days.

    What this layer can't see

    • Who holds the coins, and where. A wallet has no country. The FSB calls this a structural limit, and onchain data doesn't fix it.
    • The reserves themselves. Which banks hold the cash, how much of it can move the same day, under which jurisdiction. That sits off-chain with the issuer.
    • Hours of cover. The number a supervisor would want is how many hours of redemptions, at the current pace, the issuer's same-day liquidity covers. This page measures the pace. The other half needs the issuer to report liquid reserves privately, and the two can then be joined.

    Method

    • Source: Google's public Ethereum dataset, bigquery-public-data.crypto_ethereum.token_transfers. One query, run for each window.
    • Mints and burns: USDC transfers from and to the zero address. Ethereum only; USDC on other chains isn't included.
    • Pool flows: USDT transfers into and out of Curve 3pool (0xbebc…f1c7), net per period. They include liquidity adds and removals, not only swaps.
    • Swap price: per transaction, USDT out of the pool divided by USDC into it, for transactions selling more than 1,000 USDC. Transactions with a ratio outside 0.5–1.5 are dropped as multi-leg routes. Median per period.
    Data: Ethereum mainnet via Google BigQuery public datasets.
